# BFG Source Lineage and Contribution Record

## Balance–Field Framework (BFG)

This document records the intellectual and formal source lineage of the **Balance–Field Framework (BFG)**.

Its purpose is to preserve a clear distinction between:

* framework authorship,
* early conceptual contribution,
* Level-0 contribution,
* formal source-line contribution,
* co-authorship,
* methodological review,
* implementation contribution,
* and later independent development.

Scientific provenance and authorship are not treated as interchangeable.

A contribution to one part of the BFG does not automatically imply authorship of the entire framework, and later framework development does not erase earlier documented contributions.

# 4. Source-Line Separation Principle

BFG uses a source-line separation principle.

A documented contribution should be credited at the level at which it actually enters the framework.

The following categories are therefore kept distinct:

### Framework authorship

Authorship and responsibility for the overall BFG architecture and its continuing development.

### Early conceptual contribution

An idea, question, critique, or conceptual impulse that materially influenced a later BFG development.

### Level-0 contribution

A contribution specifically related to the admissible origin structure preceding later BFG closure architecture.

### Formal source-line contribution

A specific mathematical or operator construction that enters the BFG corpus.

### Co-authorship

Joint authorship of a specific document or formally co-authored result.

### Methodological review

Criticism, review, correction, or methodological feedback that improves a BFG construction without automatically creating authorship of the resulting framework.

### Implementation contribution

Code, simulation infrastructure, tooling, data processing, or another executable implementation contribution.

### Later independent development

Subsequent BFG structures developed independently from earlier source-line contributions.

---

# 5. Contribution Does Not Imply Total Authorship

A contribution to one source line does not automatically transfer authorship of later BFG developments.

For example:

* contribution to Level 0 does not imply authorship of all later BFG mathematics,
* contribution to a recursive operator does not imply authorship of all BFG simulations or domain applications,
* methodological review does not automatically imply authorship of the reviewed theory,
* implementation work does not automatically imply authorship of the theoretical framework.

Likewise, later authorship does not erase an earlier documented conceptual or formal contribution.

The guiding principle is:

> **Source credit should be specific enough to preserve provenance without expanding a contribution beyond the work actually documented.**

---

# 8. Scientific Responsibility

Contribution credit and responsibility for scientific claims are separate questions.

A contributor should not automatically be treated as responsible for later claims that they did not author, approve, or participate in developing.

Accordingly:

**credit should follow contribution**

and

**claim responsibility should follow the relevant authored work.**

This distinction is particularly important in a framework that develops through multiple papers, simulations, domain reconstructions, and methodological revisions.

---

# 9. Provenance and Versioning

Changes to this source-lineage record should be versioned rather than silently overwritten.

A substantive modification should state:

* what attribution changed,
* why it changed,
* what documentary basis supports the change,
* and which BFG work or source line is affected.

Historical contribution records should not be removed merely because later formulations become more developed.

Where uncertainty exists, the status should remain explicitly open until documentary support is available.

---

# 10. Boundary Against Unsupported Attribution

This document should not be used to infer contributions that are not supported by the BFG source record.

In particular:

* names should not be added merely because a person discussed BFG,
* review should not automatically be converted into co-authorship,
* conceptual similarity should not be converted into contribution without documented contact or source lineage,
* later mathematical developments should not be retroactively attributed to earlier contributors without evidence,
* repository licensing should not be treated as authorship transfer.

The objective is accurate scientific provenance rather than maximal attribution.
