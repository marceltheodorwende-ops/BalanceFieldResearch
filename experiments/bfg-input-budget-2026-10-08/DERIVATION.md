## Derivation for general Hermitian inputs
1. On the prepared full-persistence domain, positive loads/full rank/empty complement ensure each event nonterminal. P47 applies to Z_k even when F and Z_k do not commute: M_(k+1)≤||Z_k||/2.
2. Triangle inequality gives M_(k+1)≤(M_k+delta_k)/2. Equivalently delta_k≥2M_(k+1)-M_k. Negative right side is allowed and may yield only a vacuous bound.
3. Sum N events: sum_(k=0)^(N-1) delta_k≥2M_N-M_0+sum_(k=1)^(N-1) M_k. If every endpoint M_1,...,M_N≥m>0, then total input≥(N+1)m-M_0. Initial M_0 need not exceed m.
4. Reservoir feasibility imposes total input≤r_0/c=B. Consequently N≤(M_0+B)/m-1. The integer horizon must also satisfy N≥0; if the right side is negative there is no positive-length feasible reserve trajectory. This is NECESSARY, not a sufficient viability certificate or claim that arbitrary bound-attaining controls exist.
5. Iteration gives M_N≤2^-N M_0+sum_(k=0)^(N-1)2^(-(N-k))delta_k. If total input is finite, the convolution tends to zero: split at a fixed index J; the finite prefix vanishes geometrically and the tail is bounded by its summable norm. Thus any infinite admissible trajectory with finite reservoir has M_k→0. It may remain nonterminal for every finite k; convergence to boundary is not finite termination.
6. If instead a declared squared cost c delta_k² has finite total, delta_k→0. Split convolution using tail supremum to obtain the same M_k→0; under reserve, Cauchy–Schwarz gives B_2≥max(0,(N+1)m-M_0)²/N for N>0. This alternative demonstrates dependence of horizon law on the added cost hypothesis, not unique physical cost.

A uniformly positive minimum eigenvalue/load reserve is stronger than M_k≥m and therefore also cannot persist with finite input budget. Maintaining only an upper-norm reserve does not guarantee any lower eigenvalue, load gap or numerical regularity.
