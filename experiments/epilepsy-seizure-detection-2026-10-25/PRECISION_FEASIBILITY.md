## Conditional conservative bound, with proof
Let X_p=S_ap-S_bp in[-1,1] be paired per-person sensitivities for independently sampled target-population people with well-defined positive event counts. This is an EQUAL-PERSON estimand. Zero-event people do not have defined S_p; selecting event-positive people changes the target population and must be stated. Do not reuse this bound for the pooled event-weighted ratio in patient_comparison.py or treat multiple seizures as independent people.

For any tilted law of X in[-1,1], Var(X)<=1: Var(X)<=E[(X-0)^2]<=1. For the centered log-MGF psi(t)=log E exp(t(X-E X)), boundedness permits differentiation, psi(0)=psi'(0)=0 and psi''(t)=Var_t(X)<=1. Twice integrating gives psi(t)<=t^2/2. Independence and Markov's inequality give P(mean(X)-Emean(X)>=r)<=exp(-n*r^2/2) after optimizing t=r; the same argument for-X and a union bound gives P(|mean(X)-Emean(X)|>=r)<=2exp(-n*r^2/2). Thus r=sqrt(2log(2/alpha)/n) is a sufficient two-sided radius. Intersect an interval with[-1,1]; never treat its raw radius as a sensitivity outside physical range. This is a conservative sufficient bound, not a lower bound on achievable precision, not a sample-size impossibility theorem and not a fixed clinical inference choice.

Illustration alpha=.05, with no observations: n5 radius1.214723; n14 .725936; n18 .640215. These do NOT justify declaring a narrow noninferiority margin met. Radius.05/.10/.20 requires n>=2952/738/185 under THIS bound. These radii are arithmetic illustrations, not selected clinical margins, recommended trial sizes or an estimate of required sample size under better justified models. Heterogeneity, selection, dependence or domain shift can invalidate the premise. Existing development participants are not untouched confirmation data.

## False-activation rates: finite operational bounds vs unsupported tail assumptions
If sample-end activation receipts permit at most one activation at each of n=T*f sample ends, then F/T<=f and F*86400/T<=B=86400*f. This is an operational counting bound, not a neural law and not a new refractory/suppression rule. At256Hz B=22118400/day: mathematically finite but extremely loose. A paired per-person rate difference is in[-B,B], so the previous proof after scaling gives radius B*sqrt(2log(2/alpha)/n). The numerical audit records this large conservative radius. Actual stricter alarm interval constraints can improve the combinatorial bound, but no clinically useful cap is silently imposed or fitted. Full recorded time, rather than ready-only time, is necessary for this bound.

For genuinely UNBOUNDED nonnegative rate laws, no uniformly valid finite upper-mean confidence bound can be inferred from observing only zeros without tail assumptions: fix n,alpha and a procedure returning finite U on the all-zero sample. Choose epsilon>0 small enough that(1-epsilon)^n>alpha; take P(X=0)=1-epsilon and P(X=M)=epsilon with M>max(U,0)/epsilon. The mean exceeds U while the all-zero sample has probability>alpha. Therefore uniform coverage fails. This counterexample does NOT override the finite sample-clock bound above. It identifies the scope of the unbounded-distribution obstruction, not universal impossibility of precision estimation or a claim that real EEG has such a law.
