## 2. Scalar feedback is a different mathematical object

The April 17, 2025 formulation, Section 7.2, defines the scalar/field feedback diagnostic

```math
I_B=\frac{d}{dt}(\Phi I)=I\dot\Phi+\Phi\dot I.
```

The newly checked February 4, 2026 **Core Ontology and Regime Logic**, Section 3.3, calls it a marker of directed co-variation. The supplied 2026 consolidated architecture, Sections 2–4, gives the same product derivative and explicit order-dynamics equations. Those equations are real source content and are not ignored here. However, their scalar information density I is not automatically the Hilbert-space operator hat I, and the product derivative is not a definition of hat F's action on every state.

For example, on t>=0 the two scalar paths `(Phi,I)=(1,1+t)` and `(1+t,1)` have identical initial values, the same product 1+t and the same I_B=1. Their separate time derivatives differ. This disproves uniqueness from the product-derivative identity alone, not from a fully specified scalar evolution system. Additional supplied dynamics can remove that freedom and must be checked in their stated regime.

Likewise the existing affine feedback relation `I_B=A+B I_B` gives `I_B=A/(1-B)` when B!=1, **if A and B are supplied**. It does not by itself identify a full feedback operator hat F or the primitive coupling map. The source's operator, scalar, affine-coefficient and density symbols must keep their separate types.

## 3. Exact factor-composition test

Write J=hat I and F_op=hat F to avoid confusing them with identity matrices and formation density. On a finite carrier with invertible J,

```math
R=J F_{op}J
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
F_{op}=J^{-1}R J^{-1}.
```

Thus composition alone permits any R while F_op is free. This is an algebraic statement in that declared finite sector, not an assertion that every arbitrary F_op obeys all cognitive admissibility requirements.

An exact comparison keeps `J=diag(1,2)`, `N=I`, expansive operator `E=J^(-1)` and binding operator `B=I` fixed. The cognitive balance ratio is then one for every nonzero state. The two feedback factors `F_a=diag(1,1/8)` and `F_b=diag(1,1/4)` give

```math

```

Both preserve the same first line and are power bounded, but have different persistent ranks. Their source residual `||R_N e_1-e_1||` is zero in both. This witness compares only primitive composition, balance, preservation and boundedness; it does **not** claim both solve every source neural evolution equation with the same full initial data.

Singular J is different: `rank(R)<=rank(J)`. In the positive Hermitian J sector, a representation R=J F_op J exists exactly when `R=P_J R P_J`, and one solution is `F_op=J^+ R J^+`. If J were rank one, invertible neutral mediation could not increase the recursion rank; a preserved formed line would then be the only possible peripheral line. But the reviewed source calls J a primitive informational coupling operator and does not impose rank one or equate J with the formed-profile projector. Installing that equation would add the missing premise, not derive it.


## 5. Normalization and conjugate mediation are not interchangeable

The May 14 definition uses `R_N=N^(-1/2) R N^(-1/2)`. The May 16 extension introduces `R_C=N^(1/2) R N^(1/2)`. For fixed N and R these are generally different operators. With `N=I/2` and `R=I`, the first is 2I and the second is I/2. They have different spectral/stability behavior. Neither may be substituted for the other while claiming all original input data and dynamics are unchanged. Their historical relationship is the explicit extension, not a harmless notation change.

This also prevents importing a spectral condition from one representative into the other without its hypotheses. It does not by itself refute either declared regime or the current finite successor rule.
