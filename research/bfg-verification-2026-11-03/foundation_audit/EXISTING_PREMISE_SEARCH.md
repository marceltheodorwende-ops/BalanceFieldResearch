## Additional check: no-retuning and balance do not silently fix the input

The earlier scalar examples already established relative freedom. We now test a stronger objection: perhaps eliminating fitted constants, fixing the neutral metric, normalizing source units and imposing reciprocal balance remove it.

Hold the same formed Hessian H=diag(2,3) and intrinsic neutral metric M=H fixed. Let R=H/tr(H). Compare the two fixed mathematical witness functions

`L1=(I+R)^-1` and `L2=((I+R)^-1)^2`.

They are functions only of the same source data, transform covariantly, and are unchanged by positive scalar rescaling of H. Neither contains a fitted coefficient. With the same completed-square assessment their normalized loads are

`Y1=diag(25/49,25/64)` and `Y2=diag(625/2401,625/4096)`.

Both are strictly between zero and identity. For the same positive formation density diag(1,2), both give positive retained/complementary loads and admit the unique reciprocal weights. Both then use the **unchanged original intrinsic-Gram successor rule**. They nonetheless have different input neutral spectra. Consequently reciprocal weighting, frame covariance and source-scale normalization alone do not choose the upstream interface.

These are logical witnesses in an explicitly weaker premise class. They are not proposed physical laws, not claims of compliance with every historical 'freedom-closed' requirement, and not counterexamples to the full L0-NC premise. A stronger no-retuning or role axiom might exclude one, but its precise statement and source implication would have to be checked. Merely requiring zero fitted parameters does not do so.
