# Balance Field Research

**Research repository for the Balance–Field Framework (BFG)**

Balance Field Research documents the development, formalization, simulation, audit, and empirical evaluation of the **Balance–Field Framework (BFG)**.

BFG is a mathematical and methodological research framework concerned with admissible difference, polarity, neutral mediation, balance and order, structured information, stable configuration, recursive closure, attractor persistence, regulated export, differentiation, distinguishability-preserving transport, and falsifiability.

A central BFG quantity is the positive, dimensionless **order parameter Φ**.

The framework is developed under explicit rules for claim-state separation, source provenance, null-model comparison, ablation, failure preservation, and no-retuning transfer.

> **Mathematical coherence, simulation success, and descriptive cross-domain fit are not treated as empirical proof.**

---

# 1. Scientific Status

**Current status:** theoretical and methodological research framework with mathematical definitions, conditional theorem-level claims, ansatz-level constructions, simulation models, operationalization proposals, and explicit falsification gates.

The strongest current BFG claim is methodological:

> **BFG can be formulated as an auditable research framework whose claims remain conditional on explicit operational and falsification criteria.**

BFG does not currently claim:

* empirical proof of universal applicability,
* established cross-domain universality,
* a confirmed new fundamental law of nature,
* replacement of established physics, biology, neuroscience, chemistry, mathematics, or other mature sciences,
* that simulation constitutes empirical confirmation,
* that mathematical closure alone establishes physical truth,
* that recursive closure alone establishes consciousness,
* or that BFG terminology may override validated external evidence.

**Empirical cross-domain confirmation remains open.**

# 2. Level-0 Origin Basis

BFG begins from a **Level-0 admissibility basis**.

Level 0 is the formal condition under which distinguishable difference can be admitted before ordinary geometry, objecthood, measurement, or domain-specific mechanisms are presupposed.

The elementary conditions are:

**A0 ≠ ∅**

**Δ ⊂ A0**

**δ ∈ Δ**

**δ ≠ ∅**

Here:

* `A0` is the admissible Level-0 capacity domain,
* `Δ` is the domain of admissible differences,
* `δ` is a non-empty difference profile.

Level 0 is not itself empirical evidence.

It provides the formal starting condition for the later BFG closure architecture.

# 3. Core BFG Sequence

The compact audit sequence is:

**A0 → δ → P1/P2 → N → K → C → A → Eout/Γ → TA → F**

where:

| Symbol | BFG function                         |
| ------ | ------------------------------------ |
| A0     | admissible origin or capacity domain |
| δ      | admissible difference profile        |
| P1/P2  | distinguishable pole roles           |
| N      | neutral mediation                    |
| K      | stable profile configuration         |
| C      | recursive closure                    |
| A      | attractor persistence                |
| Eout/Γ | regulated export or residual channel |
| TA     | admissible transport                 |
| F      | falsification gate                   |

In extended BFG constructions, an explicit selection stage may also be resolved:

**A → SA → TA**

where `SA` denotes admissible selection.

More detailed Level-0 work may also resolve relation selection explicitly between polarity formation and neutral mediation.

# 4. From Difference to Polarity

A difference profile becomes closure-capable when complementary roles can be distinguished.

The compact polarity decomposition is:

**Π(δ) = (P1, P2)**

The two poles are not required to be substances or physical objects.

They are functional positions within a declared closure relation.

Depending on the carrier, they may represent different complementary roles.

Polarity alone is not closure.

A direct dyadic relation may still:

* collapse,
* dominate,
* fragment,
* oscillate,
* or amplify without stable mediation.

This motivates the BFG mediation layer.

---

# 5. Triadic Mediation

The central triadic role structure is:

**P1 — N — P2**

`P1` and `P2` remain distinguishable.

`N` supplies the neutral mediating role.

The mediator must be **load-bearing**.

Simply assigning the label `N` to a variable does not demonstrate BFG mediation.

A basic mediator ablation is:

**N → 0**

If removal, replacement, randomization, or shuffling of `N` produces no relevant loss, the stronger BFG mediation claim is weakened.

The mediated architecture should therefore be compared with a direct alternative:

**P1 — P2**

versus:

**P1 — N — P2**

If an appropriately matched dyadic model performs equally well, the triadic interpretation is not supported merely by terminology.

---

# 6. Stable Configuration

The compact BFG configuration is:

**K = ClN(P1, P2, Eout)**

where:

* `P1` and `P2` are the declared pole roles,
* `N` is the mediator,
* `Eout` is regulated export,
* `K` is the resulting stable profile configuration.

In words:

> **A BFG configuration is a mediated closure of distinguishable pole roles under a declared export condition.**

---

# 7. Recursive Closure

The configuration enters recursive closure through:

**C = RC(K)**

For iterative dynamics:

**C(t + 1) = RC(C(t))**

`RC` denotes the recursive closure operator.

Momentary balance is therefore not sufficient.

A candidate persistent system must preserve its relevant organizational regime under repeated transformation.

---

# 8. Attractor Persistence

A persistent recursive regime may be represented by:

**A = Fix(RC)**

`A` denotes an attractor or fixed regime of the declared recursive dynamics.

An attractor alone is not proof of BFG closure.

A trivial collapsed state can also be an attractor.

BFG therefore evaluates persistence together with:

* differentiation,
* distinguishability,
* bounded recursion,
* mediation,
* export,
* and transport.

---

# 9. Conjugate Recursive Closure

A recursive stability refinement used in the BFG corpus is:

**RC = M · R · M†**

with:

**M = √N**

Therefore:

**RC = √N · R · (√N)†**

where:

| Symbol | Meaning                                     |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------- |
| R      | raw recursive propagation                   |
| N      | neutral admissibility or mediation operator |
| M      | square-root neutral interface               |
| M†     | adjoint of M                                |
| RC     | mediated recursive closure operator         |

This construction is a formal stability refinement.

It does not imply that every empirical carrier must instantiate exactly the same operator representation.

# 10. The BFG Order Parameter Φ

**Status: BFG Core Structure + Mathematical Definition**

The reduced BFG order parameter is positive:

**Φ > 0**

The standard physical form is:

**Φ = (Eexp · I) ÷ (Ebind · I0)**

where:

| Symbol | Meaning                                     |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------- |
| Φ      | dimensionless BFG order parameter           |
| Eexp   | expansive or dispersive drive               |
| Ebind  | binding or stabilizing drive                |
| I      | structured physically effective information |
| I0     | reference information scale                 |

The BFG corpus specifies:

**Eexp > 0**

**Ebind > 0**

**I ≥ 0**

**I0 > 0**

Dimensional consistency requires:

**[Eexp] = [Ebind]**

and

**[I] = [I0]**

therefore:

**[Φ] = 1**

The order parameter Φ is dimensionless.

---

# 11. Space-Time Form of Φ

For a spatially and temporally varying regime:

**Φ(x,t) = (Eexp(x,t) · I(x,t)) ÷ (Ebind(x,t) · I0(x,t))**

In reduced formulations, `I0` may instead be treated as a fixed reference scale:

**Φ(x,t) = (Eexp(x,t) · I(x,t)) ÷ (Ebind(x,t) · I0)**

The notation must be declared before interpretation.

---

# 12. Domain-Specific Form of Φ

For a domain `d`, the cross-domain BFG form is:

**Φd = (Eexp,d · Id) ÷ (Ebind,d · I0,d)**

where:

* `Eexp,d` denotes the expansive, dispersive, generative, exploratory, or destabilizing tendency of domain `d`,
* `Ebind,d` denotes its binding, stabilizing, constraining, or structure-preserving tendency,
* `Id` denotes structured information available in the domain,
* `I0,d` denotes the declared reference information scale.

This is the form used in the cross-domain BFG invariant candidate.

---

# 13. Order Regimes

The fundamental BFG regime classification is:

**Φ < 1 → binding-dominated regime**

**Φ = 1 → balanced dynamic regime**

**Φ > 1 → expansion-dominated regime**

For empirical systems, the balance corridor may be expressed approximately:

**Φ ≈ 1**

The balanced regime does not mean static equilibrium.

A dynamically active system may remain near the balance corridor.

Likewise:

* `Φ < 1` does not automatically imply successful stability,
* `Φ > 1` does not automatically imply emergence,
* `Φ ≈ 1` does not by itself establish recursive closure.

Φ is therefore a **balance diagnostic**, not a standalone confirmation variable.

---

# 14. Information–Order Feedback

The BFG information–order feedback operator is:

**IB = ∂t(ΦI)**

For an ordinary time-dependent system:

**IB = d(ΦI) ÷ dt**

Applying the product rule:

**IB = I · dΦ/dt + Φ · dI/dt**

The feedback can therefore change because:

* Φ changes,
* I changes,
* or both change simultaneously.

For spatially extended systems, the corpus also uses:

**IB(x,t) = ∂t(ΦI) + ∇Φ · JI**

where `JI` denotes information flux.

---

# 15. Recursive Information Feedback

A minimal affine recursive feedback form is:

**IB = A + B · IB**

Solving for `IB` gives:

**IB = A ÷ (1 − B)**

The feedback-critical boundary is:

**1 − B = 0**

An admissible implementation should therefore remain separated from that singular boundary:

**|1 − B| ≥ ε**

with:

**ε > 0**

Approaching the critical boundary is not automatically interpreted as emergence.

Depending on the carrier and dynamics, it may indicate:

* runaway amplification,
* instability,
* fragmentation,
* collapse,
* or another transition.

---

# 16. Recursive Stability

The BFG closure hypothesis requires more than repeated transformation.

A persistent closure regime is expected to remain close to a bounded spectral regime:

**ρ(RC) ≈ 1**

A bounded tolerance formulation may also be used:

**ρ(RC) ≤ 1 + δs**

Repeated recursive propagation must remain finite:

**sup ‖RCⁿs‖ < ∞**

This means that repeated application of `RC` must not produce unrestricted runaway divergence.

But boundedness alone is not sufficient.

A system may remain bounded because it has collapsed.

Therefore recursive stability must be evaluated together with non-collapse differentiation.

---

# 17. Regulated Export

BFG includes a regulated export or residual channel:

**Eout**

or:

**Γ**

Export represents controlled handling of excess that cannot be indefinitely integrated into the current closure.

A relevant intervention is:

**Eout → 0**

If blocking the proposed export channel produces none of the predicted changes in:

* burden,
* overload,
* drift,
* instability,
* fragmentation,
* transition,
* or loss of persistence,

then the stronger BFG export claim is weakened.

An ordinary output is not automatically equivalent to regulated BFG export.

Its functional role must be demonstrated.

---

# 18. Differentiation and Non-Collapse

Boundedness alone cannot distinguish organized persistence from trivial collapse.

The cross-domain BFG formulation therefore uses a differentiation measure `Dd`.

The normalized participation ratio is:

**Dd = (Σ xi²)² ÷ (N · Σ xi⁴)**

with:

**0 ≤ Dd ≤ 1**

The non-collapse condition is:

**Dd > Dmin**

Low `Dd` indicates concentration, collapse, or loss of differentiated organization.

Higher `Dd` indicates more distributed structure.

The exact interpretation of `Dd` remains carrier-dependent and must be operationalized before confirmatory testing.

---

# 19. Order-Maintenance Capacity

Balance and closure alone are not sufficient to characterize every form of organized persistence.

The cross-domain BFG architecture therefore introduces an order-maintenance capacity:

**Ad = Omaint,d ÷ (Odecay,d + ε)**

where:

* `Omaint,d` is the capacity to maintain, regenerate, repair, or stabilize organization,
* `Odecay,d` is the tendency toward decay, disorder, fragmentation, dispersion, or loss,
* `ε > 0` prevents division by zero.

The basic regimes are:

**Ad < 1 → decay dominates**

**Ad ≈ 1 → maintenance regime**

**Ad > 1 → active order maintenance dominates**

`Ad` must be interpreted according to the domain.

For a passive physical system it must not be reinterpreted as metabolism, consciousness, or biological agency.

# 21. Domain Recursive Closure

For a domain `d`, the mediated recursive operator is:

**RC,d = Md · Rd · Md†**

with:

**Md = √Nd**

where:

* `Rd` is the raw domain propagation operator,
* `Nd` is the domain admissibility or mediation operator,
* `Md` is the corresponding square-root interface.

The closure condition is:

**ρ(RC,d) ≈ 1**

and bounded propagation requires:

**sup ‖RC,dⁿ sd‖ < ∞**

Stable organization therefore requires neither unrestricted decay nor runaway divergence.

It requires bounded persistence without erasing relevant differentiation.

# 22. Extended Persistence Corridor

The cross-domain corpus also defines a broader diagnostic corridor:

**CBFG(d) = ( |log Φd|, |log ρ(RC,d)|, Ad, Dd, Gd )**

where `Gd` measures late-time dynamical trend.

A basic trend quantity is:

**Gd(t) = d log ‖sd(t)‖ ÷ dt**

Sustained positive growth may indicate runaway.

A stable regime therefore requires a declared upper trend bound.

The extended corridor supplements the compact invariant `KBFG(d)`; it does not replace it.

# 23. Empirical Operationalization of Φ

**Status: Empirical Hypothesis / Operationalization Requirement**

For empirical applications, Φ must be computed from declared estimators rather than inferred retrospectively from whether a system appears stable.

A generic estimator is:

**Φ̂R(t) = (Êexp,R(t) · ÎR(t)) ÷ (Êbind,R(t) · I0,R + ηR)**

where:

**ηR > 0**

is a declared regularization term.

Before confirmatory evaluation, the following should be frozen:

* the `Eexp` estimator,
* the `Ebind` estimator,
* the `I` estimator,
* the reference scale `I0`,
* normalization,
* regularization,
* thresholds,
* feature definitions,
* and decision rules.

If these are altered after seeing the target result in order to obtain the desired classification, the corresponding no-retuning claim fails.

# 25. Claim-State Discipline

Every substantial BFG statement should be assigned an explicit epistemic status.

| Claim State                      | Meaning                                                              |
| -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Level-0 Origin Basis**         | origin or admissible foundation within BFG lineage                   |
| **BFG Core Structure**           | stable framework architecture and role grammar                       |
| **Mathematical Definition**      | declared formula, operator, metric, predicate, or formal object      |
| **Theorem-Level Claim**          | result following from stated definitions and assumptions             |
| **Ansatz-Level Construction**    | proposed structure awaiting operational testing                      |
| **Simulation / Model**           | computational realization or synthetic scaffold                      |
| **Empirical Hypothesis**         | externally measurable claim requiring data                           |
| **Established External Science** | independently established result used as external anchor or boundary |
| **Speculative Extension**        | proposal beyond present support                                      |
| **Redescription-Only**           | mapping that adds no demonstrated constraint or predictive value     |
| **Rejected / Failed**            | claim that did not pass its declared gate                            |

A theorem-level statement is valid only under its assumptions.

A simulation is not empirical proof.

An empirical hypothesis requires external measurement.

A speculative extension does not become validated through internal coherence alone.

# 27. Falsification Discipline

BFG is designed so that substantive claims can fail.

Strong empirical claims require, where applicable:

1. declared operational variables,
2. dimensional consistency,
3. matched null models,
4. mediator and role ablations,
5. recursive boundedness tests,
6. differentiation preservation,
7. distinguishability preservation,
8. export-channel testing,
9. scaling tests,
10. frozen held-out evaluation,
11. no-retuning transfer,
12. preservation of negative results,
13. independent replication appropriate to the strength of the claim.

Important failure conditions include the following.

## Mediator Failure

**N → 0**

with no meaningful structural or performance loss.

## Dyadic Equivalence

A matched:

**P1 — P2**

model performs as well as:

**P1 — N — P2**

## Export Failure

**Eout → 0**

produces none of the predicted consequences.

## Recursive Failure

The proposed closure operator does not improve the declared target relative to an appropriate simpler recursive alternative.

## Differentiation Failure

The system remains bounded while:

**Dd ≤ Dmin**

## Transfer Failure

A new carrier can only be accommodated after changing the BFG test grammar.

## Null-Model Failure

A properly matched simpler alternative performs equally well or better.

When a declared gate fails, the corresponding BFG claim must be weakened, restricted, or rejected.

# 28. Redescription Null Model

One of the principal BFG null hypotheses is **redescription-only**.

Examples include:

**existing interaction → “mediation”**

**existing stability → “closure”**

**existing output → “export”**

**existing attractor → “BFG attractor”**

If BFG only replaces existing terminology without adding:

* new constraints,
* measurable consequences,
* ablation sensitivity,
* failure localization,
* predictive discrimination,
* or transferable structure,

then BFG-specific scientific added value has not been demonstrated.

The decisive question is not whether BFG can describe many systems.

The stronger question is whether BFG contributes testable structure beyond simpler alternatives.

---

# 29. No-Retuning Principle

Cross-domain claims require a strict distinction between **carrier change** and **framework retuning**.

Different domains may instantiate BFG roles through different mechanisms.

However, a cross-domain claim is weakened when the underlying:

* definitions,
* operators,
* estimators,
* thresholds,
* decision rules,
* scoring architecture,
* or failure criteria

must be rewritten after observing each new result.

The general principle is:

> **The carrier may change. The test grammar must not be rewritten after seeing the answer.**

No-retuning separates genuine transfer from post-hoc accommodation.

---

# 30. Failure Preservation

Negative, null, contradictory, and inconclusive results are scientifically relevant outputs.

A failed claim should be:

* retained,
* assigned a claim state,
* connected to its relevant test,
* interpreted inside its declared failure boundary,
* and not silently rewritten as success.

Failure preservation is not intended to protect BFG from criticism.

It is intended to protect the research process from moving-target interpretation.

---

# 31. Relationship to Established Science

BFG does not override validated domain knowledge.

A domain application should distinguish between:

1. **established external science,**
2. **BFG reconstruction of that science,**
3. **additional BFG-specific predictions or constraints.**

In mature empirical domains, external scientific constraints remain binding.

A BFG interpretation becomes scientifically meaningful only when it remains compatible with the relevant evidence or produces a clearly stated, independently testable disagreement.

**Ontology cannot rescue a failed operational result.**

# 37. Scientific Boundary

Balance Field Research documents an active scientific research program.

Formal closure is not equivalent to empirical confirmation.

Simulation success is not equivalent to natural-law validation.

Cross-domain analogy is not equivalent to a universal invariant.

**Φ ≈ 1** is not by itself evidence of successful BFG closure.

Recursive boundedness is not sufficient if differentiation collapses.

A triadic labeling is not evidence of mediation unless `N` is operationally load-bearing.

An output is not evidence of regulated export unless its removal produces the predicted consequence.

A cross-domain mapping is not evidence of universality if its rules must be rewritten for every new carrier.

Empirical support requires, according to the strength and domain of the claim:

* measured data,
* appropriate controls,
* matched null models,
* causal ablations,
* frozen evaluation,
* scaling tests,
* no-retuning transfer,
* robustness analysis,
* failure preservation,
* and independent replication.

The decisive scientific question is therefore not:

> **Can BFG describe the result?**

but:

> **Does BFG impose testable constraints that survive serious alternatives while retaining a clear route to failure?**
