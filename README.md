# Balance Field Research

**Official research repository for the Balance–Field Framework (BFG)**

Balance Field Research documents the development, formalization, simulation, audit, and empirical evaluation of the **Balance–Field Framework (BFG)**.

BFG is a mathematical and methodological research framework concerned with admissible difference, polarity, neutral mediation, stable configuration, recursive closure, attractor persistence, regulated export, distinguishability-preserving transport, and falsifiability.

The framework is developed under explicit claim-state, provenance, null-model, ablation, and no-retuning rules.

**Mathematical coherence, simulation success, and cross-domain applicability by description are not treated as empirical proof.**

---

## Scientific Status

**Current status:** formal theoretical and methodological research framework with mathematical definitions, conditional theorem-level results, ansatz-level constructions, executed simulation models, operationalization proposals, and explicit falsification gates.

The strongest current BFG claim is methodological:

> BFG can be formulated as an auditable research framework whose claims remain conditional on explicit operational and falsification criteria.

The framework does **not** currently claim:

* empirical proof of universal applicability,
* established cross-domain universality,
* a confirmed new fundamental law of nature,
* replacement of established domain sciences,
* that simulation constitutes empirical confirmation,
* that mathematical closure alone establishes physical truth,
* that recursive closure alone establishes consciousness,
* or that BFG terminology may override validated external evidence.

Empirical cross-domain confirmation remains open.

## Level-0 Origin Basis

BFG begins from a **Level-0 admissibility basis**.

Level 0 is not treated as empirical evidence by itself. It provides the origin grammar from which later BFG roles are reconstructed.

The minimal audit spine is:

```text
A₀
→ δ
→ P₁ / P₂
→ N
→ K
→ C
→ A
→ E_out / Γ
→ T_A
→ F
```

where:

* `A₀` — admissible origin or capacity domain
* `δ` — admissible non-empty difference
* `P₁ / P₂` — distinguishable pole roles
* `N` — neutral mediation
* `K` — stable profile configuration
* `C` — recursive closure
* `A` — attractor persistence
* `E_out / Γ` — regulated export or residual channel
* `T_A` — admissible transport
* `F` — falsification gate

In extended BFG constructions, an explicit admissible selection layer `S_A` may be placed between attractor persistence and transport:

```text
A → S_A → T_A
```

Likewise, more detailed origin reconstructions may explicitly resolve relation selection before neutral mediation.

These are refinements of the core architecture rather than evidence for empirical universality.

## Core Closure Formalism

A compact BFG configuration is written as

$$
K = Cl_N(P_1,P_2,E_{out})
$$

with recursive closure

$$
C = R_C(K)
$$

and attractor persistence

$$
A = Fix(R_C).
$$

A recursive stability refinement used in the BFG corpus is

$$
R_C = MRM^\dagger,
\qquad
M=\sqrt{N}.
$$

A candidate recursively stable regime should satisfy bounded propagation conditions such as

$$
\sup_n \|R_C^n s\| < \infty
$$

together with an appropriate spectral condition such as

$$
\rho(R_C)\leq 1+\delta_s.
$$

Boundedness alone is not sufficient.

A configuration that remains bounded only because all relevant distinctions have collapsed does not satisfy the stronger BFG closure requirement.

Recursive persistence must therefore be evaluated together with differentiation and distinguishability preservation.

## Triadic Mediation

A central BFG structural requirement is that stable polarity is not treated as a sufficient dyadic construction.

The generic triadic role structure is

$$
(P_1,P_2,N).
$$

`P₁` and `P₂` remain distinguishable while `N` provides a mediating role through which an admissible configuration may form.

The mediator is required to be **load-bearing**.

If removal, replacement, or shuffling of `N` produces no relevant loss relative to matched alternatives, the BFG mediation claim is weakened or rejected for that carrier.

Neutral mediation is therefore an operational hypothesis, not merely a label.

## Cross-Domain Invariant Candidate

A current BFG candidate for the characterization of organized persistence is

$$
K_{\mathrm{BFG}}(d)
=
(\Phi_d,R_{C,d},A_d,D_d),
$$

where:

* `Φ_d` — balance parameter,
* `R_C,d` — recursive closure operator,
* `A_d` — order-maintenance capacity,
* `D_d` — differentiation / non-collapse measure.

This object is a **candidate cross-domain invariant architecture**.

It is not an empirically established universal invariant.

Its scientific relevance depends on whether operationalized versions can distinguish organized persistence from matched alternatives, localize failure, survive ablation, and transfer without arbitrary post-hoc retuning.

## Claim-State Discipline

Every substantial BFG claim should be assigned an explicit epistemic status.

| Claim State                      | Meaning                                                                 |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Level-0 Origin Basis**         | Origin or admissible foundation within BFG lineage                      |
| **BFG Core Structure**           | Stable framework architecture and role grammar                          |
| **Mathematical Definition**      | Declared formula, operator, metric, predicate, or formal object         |
| **Theorem-Level Claim**          | Result following from explicitly stated definitions and assumptions     |
| **Ansatz-Level Construction**    | Proposed structure awaiting operational testing                         |
| **Simulation / Model**           | Computational realization or synthetic scaffold                         |
| **Empirical Hypothesis**         | Externally measurable claim requiring data                              |
| **Established External Science** | Independently established result used as an external anchor or boundary |
| **Speculative Extension**        | Proposal beyond present support                                         |
| **Redescription-Only**           | Mapping that adds no demonstrated constraint or predictive value        |
| **Rejected / Failed**            | Claim that did not pass its declared gate                               |

A theorem-level statement is valid only within its assumptions.

A simulation is not empirical proof.

An empirical hypothesis requires external measurement.

A speculative extension does not become part of the validated BFG core merely through internal consistency.

## BFG Admissibility Standard

A strong BFG claim is not admitted solely because BFG terminology can be mapped onto a system.

A compact audit predicate is:

$$
Adm_{\mathrm{BFG}}(x,d)=1
$$

only when the relevant requirements are satisfied, including:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&ClaimState \\
{}\land{}&Roles \\
{}\land{}&OperationalReadout \\
{}\land{}&Distinguishability \\
{}\land{}&Recursion \\
{}\land{}&Differentiation \\
{}\land{}&NullSeparation \\
{}\land{}&NoRetuning \\
{}\land{}&FailureLedger.
\end{aligned}
$$

The exact operational realization remains domain-dependent and must respect established constraints of the domain being studied.

## Falsification Discipline

BFG is designed so that its claims can fail.

Strong empirical claims require, where applicable:

1. declared operational variables,
2. dimensional consistency,
3. matched null models,
4. role and mediator ablations,
5. recursive boundedness tests,
6. differentiation preservation,
7. distinguishability preservation,
8. export-channel testing,
9. scaling tests,
10. frozen held-out evaluation,
11. no-retuning transfer,
12. preservation of negative results,
13. independent replication for strong empirical confirmation.

A BFG-specific interpretation is weakened when a simpler matched model reproduces the same result.

Examples of relevant failure conditions include:

* mediator removal produces no meaningful loss;
* a direct dyadic model matches the triadic construction;
* export removal produces no predicted instability or performance loss;
* recursive closure does not outperform raw or simpler recursion;
* relevant distinctions collapse despite bounded dynamics;
* results disappear under held-out evaluation;
* transfer succeeds only after domain-specific post-hoc retuning;
* strong null models match or outperform the BFG construction.

Failed tests must not be reclassified as confirmation after the result is known.

---

## Redescription Null

One of the principal BFG null hypotheses is **redescription-only**.

A reconstruction fails to establish BFG-specific added value when it merely renames already-known structures:

```text
existing interaction → "mediation"
existing stability   → "closure"
existing output      → "export"
existing attractor   → "BFG attractor"
```

without introducing additional constraints, measurable consequences, failure localization, ablation sensitivity, or transferable predictions.

The burden is therefore not to demonstrate that BFG can describe many systems.

The burden is to determine whether the BFG architecture adds testable structure beyond simpler alternatives.

---

## No-Retuning Principle

Cross-domain claims require a strict distinction between **carrier change** and **framework retuning**.

Different domains may instantiate BFG roles through different physical or informational carriers.

However, a universal or cross-domain claim is weakened when the underlying decision rule, operator definition, thresholds, or scoring architecture must be changed after each new result is observed.

The general principle is:

> **The carrier may change. The test grammar must not be rewritten after seeing the answer.**

No-retuning is therefore a central boundary between genuine transfer and post-hoc accommodation.

---

## Failure Preservation

Negative, null, contradictory, or inconclusive results are scientifically relevant outputs.

BFG research therefore treats failure preservation as part of the framework itself.

A failed claim should be:

* retained,
* identified by claim state,
* connected to the relevant test,
* interpreted within the declared failure boundary,
* and not silently removed or rewritten as success.

The purpose of a failure record is not to protect BFG from criticism.

It is to protect the research process from moving-target interpretation.

---

## Relationship to Established Science

BFG does not override validated domain knowledge.

In mature empirical domains, established physical, biological, neuroscientific, mathematical, or other scientific constraints remain external admissibility boundaries.

A BFG reconstruction is scientifically useful only where it remains compatible with relevant evidence or generates a clearly stated, independently testable disagreement.

Ontology cannot rescue a failed operational result.

## Research Integrity

Balance Field Research follows several core methodological boundaries:

* claim state before interpretation,
* source provenance before transport,
* roles before outcome interpretation,
* operationalization before confirmation,
* null models before universality claims,
* ablation before causal attribution,
* held-out testing before confirmatory interpretation,
* no-retuning before cross-domain claims,
* failure preservation after negative results,
* established domain science before BFG ontology.

The central principle is:

> **BFG becomes scientifically stronger by preserving the conditions under which it can be wrong.**

## Scientific Boundary

Balance Field Research documents an active scientific research program.

Formal closure is not equivalent to empirical confirmation.

Simulation success is not equivalent to natural-law validation.

Cross-domain analogy is not equivalent to a universal invariant.

Empirical support requires measured data, appropriate controls, strong null models, ablations, frozen evaluation, transfer tests, robustness analysis, and replication appropriate to the strength of the claim.

The decisive question is therefore not:

> **Can BFG describe the result?**

but:

> **Does BFG impose testable constraints that survive serious alternatives while retaining a clear route to failure?**
